Year 12 · Speech analysis · 20 minutes

ArsenalofDemocracy

Franklin D. Roosevelt · 29 December 1940The US is officially neutral. How do you make a distant threat feel urgent without asking for war?

Stage 1 of 4 · Hook
The extract · FDR, fireside chat, 29 December 1940

This is not a fireside chat on war. It is a talk on national security, because the nub of the whole purpose of your President is to keep you now, and your children later, and your grandchildren much later, out of a last-ditch war for the preservation of American independence and all of the things that American independence means to you and to me and to ours.

· · ·

Never before since Jamestown and Plymouth Rock has our American civilization been in such danger as now.

· · ·

The Nazi masters of Germany have made it clear that they intend not only to dominate all life and thought in their own country, but also to enslave the whole of Europe, and then to use the resources of Europe to dominate the rest of the world.

· · ·

If Great Britain goes down, the Axis powers will control the continents of Europe, Asia, Africa, Australia, and the high seas — and they will be in a position to bring enormous military and naval resources against this hemisphere. It is no exaggeration to say that all of us, in all the Americas, would be living at the point of a gun — a gun loaded with explosive bullets, economic as well as military.

· · ·

There are also American citizens, many of them in high places, who, unwittingly in most cases, are aiding and abetting the work of these agents. I do not charge these American citizens with being foreign agents. But I do charge them with doing exactly the kind of work that the dictators want done in the United States.

· · ·

No man can tame a tiger into a kitten by stroking it. There can be no appeasement with ruthlessness. There can be no reasoning with an incendiary bomb.

· · ·

There is no demand for sending an American Expeditionary Force outside our own borders. There is no intention by any member of your Government to send such a force. You can, therefore, nail — nail any talk about sending armies to Europe as deliberate untruth.

· · ·

We must be the great arsenal of democracy. For us this is an emergency as serious as war itself. We must apply ourselves to our task with the same resolution, the same sense of urgency, the same spirit of patriotism and sacrifice as we would show were we at war.

Extracts, in speech order. Refer to the small red sentence numbers.

1

Hook

December 1940:

  • France has fallen.
  • Britain remains at war.
  • The United States is officially neutral.
  • Roosevelt wants to mobilise American economic and industrial support without committing American troops.

What can Roosevelt offer Britain without committing American soldiers? Take two quick answers.

Then the task: how does he make a distant threat feel urgent, without asking for war?

2

Close read

Pairs, using the extract above. For each, find evidence and cite the sentence numbers:

  • Scale: how large is the danger made to feel?
  • The enemy’s vocabulary: what words does the Axis get?
  • Pronouns: where does “we” begin and end?

Destination: how do scale, vocabulary and pronouns work together to turn a foreign conflict into an American security crisis?

Fear“Never before since Jamestown and Plymouth Rock has our American civilization been in such danger as now.”
3

Power and framing

Each pair finds one move that frightens and one that reassures. He sends both signals in the same breath. Why?

Fear“There can be no appeasement with ruthlessness.” Calm“There is no demand for sending an American Expeditionary Force outside our own borders.” Pivot“We must be the great arsenal of democracy. For us this is an emergency as serious as war itself.”

Push: he never names his opponents. Look at sentences 7–9. How does he accuse without accusing, and what does that do to anyone who disagrees?

4

Pressure test

Whole class. The core question:

Who belongs to Roosevelt’s democratic “we”, and who is excluded?

Extend it:

  • Can a country defend democracy abroad while denying equality at home?
  • Who actually gets to participate in the arsenal?
5

Exit line

One sentence each, on paper or the iPad:

Be ready to read yours aloud.
